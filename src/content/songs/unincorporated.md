# Song Q&A

## What's the song about?

The song's title comes from a number of things that were going on in my life at the same time as I wrote this song. I had a friend from a long time ago that I had drifted apart from, and I heard the news that she had died a couple years ago. That wasn't something I applied directly into the music, but it was something that was going on for me emotionally while I was writing it. I also spread the ashes of my dad, who died a long time ago. So there was this weird sort of type of loss that I was thinking about as I was writing this song, but it was tied to older things, not something very recent.

The song was also written for a Portland music producer group challenge, and it was specifically for Halloween. So the intention was to make something a little bit creepy or spooky sounding. Those were the three things going through my mind as I put this song together. And although there is a creepy edge to this song, it also has a sort of beauty to it at the same time. That really reflected my mental state as I was putting together this music.

## What's the title about?

Unincorporated really is about death, or being not corporeal. I liked the title because it's also the opposite of "corporate," and corporate is like having a body. Unincorporated is not having a body — hence death.

## How did it start?

I started with a chord progression that seemed kind of spooky. Then I added a bass line that had some movement to it and set a sort of creepy vibe, and some light drums to kind of tie the whole piece together. Once I had that, it was fulfilling some of the needs of the spooky challenge.

Then I looked for some vocal samples and found the French vocal samples that are featured in the piece. I thought something that would be spooky would be to pull some sounds from those vocal samples and make some slower splices out of them. These ended up sounding a lot like breathing, and that led to the whole idea of living and dying. So that tied into a lot of what my mental state was at the time.

## How did you make the breathing sounds?

If I remember correctly, what I did was pull out a small clip from one of the samples, pitch shift it down an octave, and stretch it out so that it took longer. I probably also added some reverb and other effects, and then I looped it so it sounded like it was repeating. The original clips are still part of the song too, but this makes some interludes that sound vaguely similar to those vocal clips.

## Is it spooky or beautiful?

I think the whole song has elements of spookiness and tenderness. It doesn't go all in on the spookiness. It's just a subtle sense of unease. And I hope it sounds balanced.

## How does this one fit into the arc of your work?

I think it shows that I'm getting a little bit more personal and delving into the emotional space a little bit better with my work. Last year, a lot of my work was about political unease, and I feel like I'm starting to explore a little bit more emotional themes.

## If this song were a scene in a film, what's on screen?

The cover image is a bit of a scene. It's a picture taken by my wife on a hike we went on recently, and I like how the dead leaves on the path are so dark, and above it shows the beauty of the forest and the sunlight behind it.

## The E's in the logo

One other thing I should add is that during this time I created a logo for myself, for J3, and the E's in the logo are inspired by how my dad used to actually write his E's: three horizontal lines with no vertical line connecting them. As you can see, there was a lot of introspection during this time that has been showing up in my work.
